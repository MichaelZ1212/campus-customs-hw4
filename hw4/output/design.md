# Campus Customs Design Improvements

## Visual identity

The redesign uses Yale blue (`#00356b`) as the primary brand color, a darker navy for depth, warm cream accents, restrained gray surfaces, and high-contrast white space. Georgia supplies an editorial, collegiate character for headings while a system sans-serif keeps navigation, controls, prices, and descriptions highly readable. Consistent spacing, borders, shadows, and button treatments create a recognizable design system across every route without requiring external font downloads.

This stronger identity makes the site feel established and trustworthy while preserving the familiar character customers expect from Yale-related merchandise.

## Homepage and calls to action

The homepage hero now pairs its concise “Wear your Yale story” message with a layered collage of real catalogue photography and a custom Yale-style seal. Primary and secondary calls to action lead directly to the collection and brand story. The supporting value section and full-width blue callout give visitors a clear path from brand introduction to shopping.

Using real merchandise above the fold helps customers understand the offering immediately. Clear action hierarchy reduces hesitation and encourages deeper catalogue exploration.

## Product browsing

Product images use a consistent portrait ratio and `object-fit: cover`, while each card now has a subtle border, soft elevation, category label, price row, directional cue, and “Campus pick” badge. Hovering lifts the card, gently enlarges the photograph, and reveals the detail action. The grid adapts from three columns to two and then one as the viewport narrows.

The visual consistency makes a large catalogue easier to scan, while prominent names and prices help shoppers compare options quickly. Restrained motion signals clickability without distracting from the merchandise.

## Product detail experience

The detail layout presents a large, sticky product image beside a distinct purchase-information panel. Description, price, colors, size inventory, and purchase controls follow a clear hierarchy. Available sizes are selectable, selected stock is summarized, zero-stock sizes are visibly disabled, and a short assurance list reinforces live inventory and secure account access.

This structure resembles familiar commerce patterns: shoppers can understand the item, choose a valid size, and see availability without searching around the page. The design reduces uncertainty near the purchase decision.

## Chat experience

The assistant retains the Yale palette and now uses a gradient header, distinct user/assistant bubbles, compact linked product previews, an elevated launcher, and a short scale-and-rise opening animation. Disabled and loading states remain clear. The panel is scrollable for longer restored conversations and adapts to narrow screens.

The polished presentation makes the assistant feel like part of the store rather than a separate utility, encouraging shoppers to ask questions and continue browsing from its product suggestions.

## Responsive and accessible behavior

Navigation wraps safely on tablets, catalogue and value grids collapse progressively, product detail columns stack on smaller screens, controls become full-width where appropriate, and the chat panel never exceeds the viewport. Focus outlines remain visible, interactive sizes expose pressed state, live regions announce chat and search updates, and `prefers-reduced-motion` disables nonessential animations.

These choices maintain usability across desktop, tablet, and phone screens and reduce motion for visitors who request it.
